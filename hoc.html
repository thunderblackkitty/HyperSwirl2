01 / Myanmar

A history shaped by the Bay of Bengal

Myanmar's coastline faces the Bay of Bengal and the Andaman Sea, making coastal communities particularly exposed to tropical storms.

The Department of Meteorology and Hydrology (DMH) describes two main cyclone seasons in Myanmar: the pre-monsoon period and the post-monsoon period.

Many storms form over the Bay of Bengal and may move west or northwest. Some turn north or northeast and approach the Myanmar coast. The Rakhine coast and the Ayeyarwady Delta are among the areas at risk from strong winds and storm surge.

Myanmar's cyclone seasons

  • Pre-monsoon: Mid-April to May
  • Post-monsoon: October to November
  • Mid-monsoon: Some storms form in the northern Bay of Bengal, but their usual paths may not bring them to Myanmar.

02 / Historical record

Major cyclones affecting Myanmar

Explore selected events recorded by Myanmar's Department of Meteorology and Hydrology. Figures are historical reports and may differ across sources.

1884

Sittwe cyclone

Historical record

May 14–17 · Sittwe

The DMH historical list reports more than 100 deaths and significant crop losses.

This is the earliest event in the DMH list shown on its cyclone information page. The record also reports substantial economic losses using the currency value of that period.
1936

Kyaukphyu cyclone

Rakhine

April 22–26 · Kyaukphyu

The DMH record reports about 1,000 deaths and severe losses to livestock and property.

The historical entry notes that more than 7,000 buffaloes and cattle died and that extensive economic damage was reported.
1968

Sittwe cyclone

Severe impact

May 7–10 · Sittwe

The DMH list reports 1,037 deaths and extensive damage to homes and livestock.

The entry records 57,663 houses destroyed and a storm surge of 4.25 feet. Historical figures are reproduced from the DMH record.
1975

Pathein cyclone

Ayeyarwady

May 5–7 · Pathein

The DMH record reports 303 deaths and major damage to houses and livestock.

The historical entry lists 246,700 houses destroyed and a storm surge of around 3 feet.
1978

Kyaukphyu cyclone

Rakhine

May 12–17 · Kyaukphyu

The DMH historical list reports that around 90% of Kyaukphyu was damaged.

The source records an estimated loss of 200 million kyats in the monetary terms used at the time.
2006

Cyclone Mala

Gwa

April 29 · Gwa area

The DMH list records one death and a storm surge of 4.57 feet.

Mala is one of the named cyclones in the more recent part of the DMH historical list. It affected the western coast of Myanmar.
2008

Cyclone Nargis

Major disaster

May 2–3 · Ayeyarwady Delta and Yangon region

Nargis caused catastrophic damage, with destructive winds and a deadly storm surge across low-lying coastal and delta communities.

The DMH historical list reports approximately 138,000 deaths and losses of 13 trillion kyats. Other humanitarian and international assessments use different totals and definitions. Nargis remains one of the most devastating disasters in Myanmar's modern history.
2010

Cyclone Giri

Rakhine

October 20–23 · Rakhine State

Giri brought destructive winds and heavy rain to coastal communities in Rakhine.

The DMH list reports 45 deaths and substantial economic losses. The event highlighted the risks faced by coastal settlements during the post-monsoon cyclone season.
2023

Cyclone Mocha

Very severe cyclone

May 8–14 · Landfall near Sittwe, Rakhine State

Mocha brought powerful winds, storm surge and heavy rainfall to Rakhine and surrounding areas.

The DMH historical list reports 151 deaths, 29,958 people injured or affected, and more than 30 billion kyats in damage. A World Bank comparison gives a range of fatality estimates from different reporting sources, so totals should be understood as source-dependent.

04 / Understanding impacts

What makes cyclones dangerous?

A cyclone can affect communities through several hazards at the same time, even after its strongest winds have passed.

Strong winds

Powerful winds can damage roofs, buildings, trees, power lines and transport infrastructure.

Heavy rainfall

Intense or prolonged rain can cause river flooding, flash floods and landslides, including far inland.

Storm surge

Strong winds and low pressure can push seawater towards the coast, flooding low-lying areas.

Damage to homes

Homes, schools, roads and community facilities may be damaged, disrupting daily life and services.

Unsafe water

Floodwater and saltwater can contaminate wells and drinking-water sources, increasing health risks.

Livelihood impacts

Crops, livestock, fishing equipment and local businesses can be affected, making recovery difficult.

05 / Learning from history

Knowledge and early warnings save lives.

Myanmar's cyclone history shows why reliable forecasts, clear communication and community preparedness are important before a storm reaches the coast.

What should communities do?

  • Follow official DMH forecasts and warnings.
  • Know the nearest safe shelter and evacuation route.
  • Prepare essential supplies before cyclone season.
  • Follow local authorities' instructions and avoid rumours.
  • Wait for official confirmation before returning to unsafe areas.
