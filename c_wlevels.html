Cyclone & Wind Levels

What do wind categories mean, how can a cyclone affect different areas, and what should you do when a warning arrives?

Explore wind levels Live DMH warnings

An educational guide, not a live forecast or warning.

This page is a guide, not a live alert

Cyclone warnings can change quickly. Use the latest DMH bulletin and follow instructions from local authorities; do not rely on an old post or this page to decide safety.

01 / Understanding Cyclone Strength

What do the levels mean?

In the Bay of Bengal, the regional IMD/RSMC scale groups cyclonic systems by their sustained wind speeds.

Bay of Bengal cyclone categories and approximate sustained wind speed ranges
System category Wind speed In plain language
Depression31–49 km/hOrganized rain and winds; conditions at sea can become rough.
Deep depression50–61 km/hStronger winds and heavier rain are possible near the system.
Cyclonic storm62–88 km/hDamaging gusts, downed branches, and localized flooding may occur.
Severe cyclonic storm89–117 km/hRoof and tree damage and widespread travel disruption become more likely.
Very severe cyclonic storm118–166 km/hSerious wind damage, dangerous coastal conditions, and power outages are possible.
Extremely severe cyclonic storm167–221 km/hVery destructive winds and major impacts are possible near the storm’s path.
Super cyclonic storm222 km/h+Exceptionally destructive winds; follow official instructions immediately.

These are category ranges, not a local forecast. Wind values refer to sustained winds; short gusts can be stronger.

02 / Possible Impacts

Wind is only one part of the danger

A cyclone can bring several hazards at once. Your area may be affected even if the center does not pass directly overhead.

Strong winds

Loose objects become dangerous; trees, signs, roofs, and power lines may be damaged.

Heavy rain

Flooding and landslides can happen, including inland and after strongest winds pass.

Coastal hazards

High waves and storm surge can quickly flood low-lying coastal regions.

Daily disruptions

Roads, electricity, phone services, and local routines may be interrupted.

A Past Example — Not a Current Warning

Cyclone Mocha, May 2023

On 14 May 2023, the Bay of Bengal regional centre described Mocha as an Extremely Severe Cyclonic Storm as it approached Sittwe, bringing sustained winds of 180–190 km/h and gusts up to 210 km/h. This example highlights why both storm category and local alerts matter.

Reported sustained wind 180–190 km/h Gusts up to 210 km/h
Why Location Matters

The same storm affects places differently

Local conditions matter as much as the storm category. Check which areas are named in bulletins.

Coastal communities

Strong winds and storm surge create serious danger near shorelines. Follow evacuation guidance.

River & low-lying areas

Heavy rain can raise water levels quickly, even when strongest winds are further away.

Inland communities

Rain can trigger landslides and disrupt transport or power. Keep checking updates.

03 / Safety Actions

What can you do?

Practical steps to help you prepare. Always follow local emergency instructions first.

Step 01

Before a warning

  • Know your safer places and routes.
  • Prepare water, food, torch, and power banks.
  • Secure loose items and protect documents.
Step 02

When warned

  • Check latest DMH updates and inform family.
  • Move to shelters if advised by authorities.
  • Stay away from beaches and flooded rivers.
Step 03

During & after

  • Stay indoors away from windows until safe.
  • Never walk or drive through floodwater.
  • Avoid fallen wires and wait for an all-clear.
